Act 1 / The Claim

The CRM is changing in shape,
not just in features.

Today
CRM as a workplace
A salesperson opens a CRM, looks at a board, updates fields, drafts emails, sets reminders. The CRM is where the work happens.
What's next
CRM as a control surface
The agent does the work. The user reviews, approves, rejects, gives feedback. The user steers a thing that works for them.
Act 1 / The Claim

Workplace → Control Surface.

Workplace
Today · human doing the work
  • Open a board
  • Update fields
  • Draft emails
  • Set reminders
  • Run reports
→
Control surface
What's next · human in the loop
  • Review agent output
  • Approve / decline
  • Edit and escalate
  • Give feedback
  • Steer missions
Act 1 / The Claim

Mobile as a primary surface,
not a secondary one.

If steering the work is what we do, a simple "swipe" is enough.
01
Steering is atomic. Approve, decline, edit, escalate. These are short, between-things decisions. Mobile-suited.
02
Setting up missions is deep work. Configuring agents, designing workflows, structuring data. Desktop-suited. TBD
03
Today our mobile exposes a fraction of the CRM and almost none of the agent layer. If steering becomes the main job, mobile is the load-bearing surface — not a slimmed-down companion.
Act 2 / Pillar 1 — Capture

Field case studies — SumUp with Leadbeam — document 6× increases in CRM data captured when reps shift from desktop entry to mobile voice/photo workflows.

Act 3 / The product question

We considered three directions.

01
Field-rep companion
Narrow, persona-bounded mobile experience for outside sales reps. Calendar, voice, photo, approvals.
02
Mobile foundation for the agentic vision
The device-side layer for agentic CRM. Approval feed, trust dial, brain-on-mobile, generalized across personas.
03
Build on top of Sidekick
Layer CRM-specific behaviors on Sidekick's existing chat-based agent surface. Leverage their connective tissue.
Act 3 / Option 01

Field-rep companion. Narrow, bounded, ship fast.

A focused mobile experience for outside sales reps. Calendar→CRM brief, voice capture, photo, post-meeting notes, approvals for follow-ups. Architecture is bounded by this persona's needs.
Why it's tempting
Cleanest scope. Bounded persona = bounded risk. Demo-ready with the most concrete user story we can tell tomorrow.
Act 3 / Option 02

Mobile foundation for the agentic vision.

Architected as the device-side layer for agentic CRM. Approval feed, trust dial, brain-on-mobile, missions surfaced. Generalized across personas — field reps, inside reps, managers — with the field rep as lead persona for Q2.
Why it's right
Architecturally honest about where the product is going. Mobile as a first-class agent surface from day one. Team identity = mobile experts who own the agent layer.
Act 3 / Option 03

Build on top of Sidekick.

Layer CRM-specific behaviors on Sidekick's existing connective tissue. Don't build a container — add capabilities. Sidekick is already mobile-first. Lower platform-engineering surface area for our team.
Why it's tempting
Ships faster than greenfield. Lower platform-engineering scope. Leverages an existing surface area instead of building one.

Act 3 / The disqualifier

Option 3 doesn't hold under one technical question.

Sidekick is structurally built around platform modules — board signing, monday onboarding, signed-in workflows. An outside sales rep who has never logged into monday doesn't fit cleanly inside that container.

Sidekick's chat-shaped agent model also doesn't fit long-running CRM missions. A "prep me for tomorrow's meetings" agent isn't a chat — it's a structured background mission with a state machine, deliverables, and an approval surface.

Option 3 trades a known mobile surface for an unknown one, and the team identity dissolves into Sidekick's surface.
Act 3 / Recommendation

Option 2. With a lean Option 1 slice
as our first shipped surface.

Recommended direction
Build Option 2 — the mobile foundation for the agentic CRM. Approval feed, trust dial, Brain on mobile. Architecture is general, not persona-bound. Team identity is the mobile experts who own the agent layer.

Ship a lean Option 1 slice on top — the outside-rep day (calendar brief → voice capture → approvals) — so we prove the foundation against real customer pain from week one, not quarter three.
Option 2 is the architecture. Option 1 is the first vertical slice that runs on it. We don't choose one — we choose the order.

Glance, swipe, decide.

A single feed of agent-suggested actions waiting for review.

Act 4 / Trust dial

Calmer in auto-mode. Louder when the agent wins.

A single dial calibrates how much the agent does on its own.
As trust grows, the rep moves the dial from "Ask before each action" to "Ask only on high-stakes" to "Run free, summarize." Notifications stay loud only for wins and escalations.
  • Per-agent and per-account scopes
  • Always reversible — undo any agent action within X hours
  • Success notifications use a different surface than work-needed alerts

Act 4 / Brain on mobile

We already have the pieces. Mobile connects them.

The brain isn't a new stack. It's the glue between things monday already ships.
On-the-go mobile, the Notetaker call recording, mobile voice capture — they all already exist. What's missing is the layer where they feed agents and agents feed them back.
  • Notetaker → brain: every recorded call becomes a structured memory the agents can reason over.
  • Mobile voice capture → brain: "note this" turns a 5-second thought into a first-class observation.
  • On-the-go mobile → brain: location, calendar, between-meeting context — already on the device, just not yet wired in.

Act 4 / The first concrete agent

The outside field rep's companion — our first agent.

The rep's day is where agent value is most obvious and most testable. One agent, three jobs. All three produce discrete, scoped, swipe-reviewable actions on the device where the rep already is.
01
Prep before the meeting. Pulls a brief from the brain — last touch, open items, stakeholders, risks — right before the rep walks in. On the phone, in the car, without a laptop.
02
Capture after the meeting. Voice note in, structured CRM update out — what happened, next action, risks, deal movement. The rep talks, the agent writes, the rep approves.
03
Follow up from the parking lot. Drafts the thank-you, the next-step email, the internal handoff. The rep swipes to approve before they've even started the car.

Act 5 / Main success KPI

The honest one is human-in-the-loop completion on mobile.

Main success KPI
% of agent-suggested actions reviewed and acted-on on mobile within X hours.
Captures whether the loop is actually working — agent suggests, rep responds, work moves. Time-bounded so a rep who batch-clears the feed Friday afternoon doesn't paper over a slow weekday. Baseline = a discovery item in W1–2.
Act 5 / Team KPI

Each team gets a mobile domain expert.
Every team has a mobile vision from day one.

Instead of keeping mobile in a silo, we embed each mobile engineer as the mobile domain expert inside a CRM product team. They shape that team's roadmap, review its design, and make sure mobile is in the conversation from day one — not bolted on later.
Team KPI
% of CRM features that ship with a first-class mobile story — designed, scoped, and owned with an embedded mobile expert — by end of Q2.
Outcome: every squad has a mobile-primary mindset by default. Mobile stops being a slimmed-down afterthought and becomes a first-class surface in every roadmap. The mobile team's value compounds across the group.

Act 6 / Team structure (side note)

The team-structure question is real, but it cuts across all three product options.

Whatever direction we choose, there are still two-or-three persona teams (the basics that aren't on mobile — Google sync, data import — are missing because of headcount). So we treat the team question in its own slot, and don't let it override the product question.
Act 6 / Three structural options

Three shapes for the team.

A
Stay together
Keep the mobile team intact, owning shelf + mobile-specific capabilities. Persona crews contribute via push-and-pull. Strongest fit for Option 2.
B
Distribute into persona teams
Embed mobile engineers into persona crews. Highest persona-cohesion. Risks losing the mobile-quality muscle and shared platform concerns.
C
Integrate into AgentX
Mobile becomes a strand inside the AgentX organization. Strongest alignment with the long-term direction. Premature if AgentX foundation isn't stable yet.
Act 6 / Q2 recommendation

Stay together for Q2. Phase the shift after the platform stabilizes.

Don't dissolve the team before there's a working agent-era model on mobile. The cost in dropped capabilities and torn-up shared knowledge is too high while the platform is still becoming stable.

The shape we recommend: stay together to ship the field-rep slice on top of the agentic foundation. After the loop is proven (~end of Q2), revisit team-structure with a real picture of what mobile actually does in the agent era.
Act 6 / The middle path

Worth raising — a small mobile-quality core with persona crews on the sides.

A small core team responsible for shared mobile concerns — performance, the shell, mobile-specific platform work, the things Gal does — while persona teams contribute mobile experiences inside their persona's skill set.

Harder to explain to the team than "we want full-stack growth and mobile quality," but more honest about what the team is actually doing.
Act 7 / Resourcing reality

The basics still missing on mobile are missing because of headcount.

Google sync, full data import, parts of the agent layer — these are real gaps and they are not solved by changing the team's shape. They are solved by adding capacity.

Whatever direction we pick, we need to name this clearly: the resource gap is real and it's separate from the architectural question. Plan against it.
Act 7 / Top risks

Three open assumptions to retire fast.

1
Mission shape on mobile. Does AgentX support background long-running missions on mobile? Spike in W1. If not, the trust dial and brain-on-mobile become hard.
2
Calendar → CRM matching accuracy. Target ≥70% match rate on calendar events to CRM accounts. Measure on real data in W1. If we miss, the field-rep brief slips a phase.
3
Background reliability on iOS. BGTaskScheduler is enough for the agent push patterns we want? Spike in W1–2. If not, we use server-driven push for now and document the constraint.

Act 9 / Mission & Scope

Mobile is where revenue users get the highest leverage from monday CRM and from the agent layer.

01
Capture
Voice notes, photos, locations, intent signals — captured at the moment work happens, not when reps get back to a laptop.
02
Control
Approve, decline, edit, escalate. The steering surface for agent-suggested actions and active missions.
03
Contribute
Feed the brain — observations, links, escalations, feedback loops. Mobile is the easiest place to do this in the moment.
Act 9 / Lead persona

We start with the field rep — the most acute case.

01
Biggest gap between when work happens (in the car, between meetings, at the client site) and when the CRM is open.
02
Biggest immediate ROI for capture — a field rep who skips post-meeting CRM updates is the most expensive seat we sell.
03
Architecturally generalizes. What works for field reps (capture, approve, contribute on the go) extends naturally to inside reps and managers.
